<h2 id="what-spicy-means-in-thai-cooking">What "Spicy" Means in Thai Cooking</h2>
<p>On our menu, the heat in a spicy dish comes from chili. How hot it feels depends on the chili it carries and the level you choose. Below, we explain what makes chili feel hot, how heat is measured and why a dish name alone will not tell you how spicy a plate will be.</p>
<h3>Capsaicin, the Compound Behind the Burn</h3>
<p>According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), the levels of a compound called capsaicin determine how hot a pepper feels. When you eat spicy food, the capsaicin attaches to heat-sensing receptors in your mouth.</p>
<p>NIST also points out that this is not real heat. It is a burning sensation in the mouth.</p>
<h3>Heat Is Measured on the Scoville Scale</h3>
<p>The Scoville Scale expresses how pungent or hot a pepper is, NIST explains. In our view, that is why the amount of chili in a dish matters so much to how hot it feels.</p>
<p>It is also why we let you set the level on every dish. Our guide to <strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/thai-restaurant-what-to-know-colorado-springs/">what to know before visiting a Thai restaurant</a></strong> covers the other questions first-time guests tend to have.</p>
<h3>A Dish Name Is Not a Heat Rating</h3>
<p>Names can mislead. On our menu, for example, the <strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/menu/thai-curry/">Green Curry</a></strong> description names a spicy curry paste, while the Red Curry description does not use the word spicy.</p>
<p>The safest guide is the dish description. If it lists chilies, chili sauce, chili paste or a spicy curry paste, the heat is part of the recipe.</p>
<h2 id="where-the-heat-comes-from-on-our-menu">Where the Heat Comes From on Our Menu</h2>
<p>On our menu, heat comes from four places: fresh chilies, chili sauces, chili paste and curry paste. Knowing which one a dish uses tells you what to expect. Each source shows up in a different part of the menu, from the salads and stir-fries to the curries and soups.</p>
<h3>Fresh Chilies in Our Salads</h3>
<p>Our salads are where fresh chili shows up most plainly. <strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/papaya-salad-colorado-springs/">Papaya Salad</a></strong> mixes green papaya, carrot and tomatoes with garlic, chilies, sugar, fish sauce, lime and green beans, topped with peanuts.</p>
<p><strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/larb-nam-tok-colorado-springs/">Larb Salad</a></strong> brings red onions, mint, green onions, lime juice and chilies together with toasted rice powder. Nam Tok Beef Salad, an Isaan-style dish, is drizzled with a zesty chili sauce.</p>
<h3>Chili Sauce and Chili Paste in Stir-Fries and Noodles</h3>
<p>Several stir-fries list chili in the sauce itself. <strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/menu/drunken-noodles/">Drunken Noodles</a></strong> and Drunken Woon Sen are made in a spicy chili sauce, and Spicy Basil Stir-Fry and Eggplant Delight are made with chili sauce.</p>
<p>Pad Prik Pao Seafood is stir-fried in chili paste and served with rice.</p>
<h3>Curry Paste in Coconut Curries</h3>
<img decoding="async" loading="lazy" src="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/nl-red-curry-paste-wide.jpg" alt="Red curry paste in a small white bowl beside a stalk of lemongrass" class="nl-post-img wp-image-977" width="400" height="267" />
<p>Our Green Curry and Avocado Green Curry list a spicy green curry paste, with bell peppers, bamboo shoots, carrots and fresh Thai basil leaves in coconut sauce. Red Curry and Pineapple Curry are made with red curry paste in coconut milk.</p>
<p>Red curry also appears in our House Specials. The Red Curry Seafood Combo brings shrimp, scallops, squid and mussels together in a red curry coconut milk, and the same section lists Red Curry with Roasted Duck and Red Curry with Kabocha Squash.</p>
<p>How the curries compare on heat is a question of its own, and our <strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/best-thai-curry-colorado-springs/">guide to choosing a Thai curry</a></strong> walks through all seven.</p>
<h3>Hot and Sour Soup</h3>
<p>Tom Yum is a clear hot and sour soup with mushrooms, tomatoes, onions, lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves and lime juice. If you want a soup without the hot and sour edge, Tom Kha is the one our menu describes as mild.</p>
<h2 id="dishes-on-our-menu-with-no-chili-listed">Dishes on Our Menu With No Chili Listed</h2>
<p>Many of our dishes list no chili at all. They are a place to start if you want to see what the menu lists before choosing a level. You will find them among our noodles, fried rice, stir-fries, soups and curries, and each one can still take more heat if you ask.</p>
<h3>Noodles and Fried Rice</h3>
<p><strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/menu/pad-thai/">Pad Thai</a></strong> is stir-fried rice noodles with egg and bean sprouts, topped with green onions and crushed peanuts. Thai Fried Rice is stir-fried rice with egg, onions, tomatoes, peas and carrot.</p>
<p>Neither description lists chili, and our <strong><a href="https://nakhonluangthaicos.com/best-thai-dish-for-beginners-colorado-springs/">guide to a first Thai order</a></strong> has more ideas for a gentle start.</p>
<h3>Stir-Fries Built on Ginger, Garlic or Brown Sauce</h3>
<p>Ginger Stir-Fry is made in a ginger-garlic sauce, and Cashew Nut comes in our special brown sauce. Garlic Pepper Stir-Fry is seasoned with white and black pepper, and its description lists no chili.</p>
<h3>Coconut Soup and Gentler Curries</h3>
<p>Tom Kha is a mild soup made with coconut milk, mushrooms, tomatoes, onions, lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves and lime juice. Yellow Curry and Massaman Curry are both made with potatoes and coconut milk, and neither description lists chili.</p>
<h2 id="how-heat-is-set-when-you-order">How Heat Is Set When You Order</h2>

Most of the heat comes from chilies, used fresh, in chili sauce, in chili paste or in curry paste. NIST explains that capsaicin in peppers is what creates the burning sensation.</p>
